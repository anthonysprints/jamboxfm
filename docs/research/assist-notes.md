# Sequencing and Assist implementation research

Research date: 2026-10-13. Implementation proposal, not implemented firmware.

Primary recommendation is grounded in Sloop `8fad1a15ff59d5c3ed0bfe2d7a9ae90962cf4d5e`. Felucca `1727537624390cc3f9c7dff2456b43b3a8edb005`, Ghoulbox `2f001b1f550eac695d8400f9f388c8eac6dbb4c6`, and ChoralRoot `aa5875af20e3046ee161f988c5d91d3ae66d0c74` were compared. Do not mix their similarly named functions or project formats.

## What Sloop really supplies

- Four tracks are already the right architecture: synth tracks 0–2 plus dedicated drum track 3. There are eight shared synth voices and six separate drum voices, not fourteen interchangeable voices. Four-note chords + one bass + one lead means six held synth voices, leaving two for overlapping releases; this is a voice-count fit, not a CPU guarantee. [core.h:3–16](https://github.com/isod89/sloop-fm1/blob/8fad1a15ff59d5c3ed0bfe2d7a9ae90962cf4d5e/firmware/src/core.h#L3-L16), [drums.c:3–39](https://github.com/isod89/sloop-fm1/blob/8fad1a15ff59d5c3ed0bfe2d7a9ae90962cf4d5e/firmware/src/drums.c#L3-L39).
- A synth step stores four absolute MIDI notes, NOTE/TIE/REST, velocity, per-note level and ratchet; a drum step stores 16 lanes with per-lane level and ratchet. Both are 10 bytes. Four 64-step arrays cost 2,560 bytes. Each track additionally has 64 microtiming bytes, 16 fill-condition bytes, and 24 four-byte parameter locks. [core.h:181–205](https://github.com/isod89/sloop-fm1/blob/8fad1a15ff59d5c3ed0bfe2d7a9ae90962cf4d5e/firmware/src/core.h#L181-L205).
- One musical clock uses a constant `BEAT_U = 44100 * 60` units per quarter note. `clk_beat` is whole beats and `clk_pos` the position inside the beat; a render block advances by `n * BPM` units under the internal clock. Keep this clock instead of introducing a second millisecond looper clock. [core.h:310–330](https://github.com/isod89/sloop-fm1/blob/8fad1a15ff59d5c3ed0bfe2d7a9ae90962cf4d5e/firmware/src/core.h#L310-L330), [seq.c:2630–2645](https://github.com/isod89/sloop-fm1/blob/8fad1a15ff59d5c3ed0bfe2d7a9ae90962cf4d5e/firmware/src/seq.c#L2630-L2645).
- `grid_swing` delays odd subdivisions while preserving the pair length. Sloop's 0–100 control corresponds to straight 50:50 through 75:25 swing. Triplets and whole-beat divisions do not swing. This differs from Felucca's maximum 70:30. [seq.c:393–439](https://github.com/isod89/sloop-fm1/blob/8fad1a15ff59d5c3ed0bfe2d7a9ae90962cf4d5e/firmware/src/seq.c#L393-L439).
- Sloop already builds TRIAD, 7TH, 9TH (1,3,7,9), SUS4 and POWER chords. Chord mode maps white keys to scale degrees; black keys act as chord modifiers. `chord_revoice` changes already-held notes when a black-key modifier changes. That is a separate explicit musical gesture and must not be called simply because Assist was toggled. [seq.c:194–281](https://github.com/isod89/sloop-fm1/blob/8fad1a15ff59d5c3ed0bfe2d7a9ae90962cf4d5e/firmware/src/seq.c#L194-L281), [seq.c:1427–1459](https://github.com/isod89/sloop-fm1/blob/8fad1a15ff59d5c3ed0bfe2d7a9ae90962cf4d5e/firmware/src/seq.c#L1427-L1459).
- Local keys retain the track and expanded notes they started in `kb_trk`, `kb_n`, `kb_nt`, then release those notes on key-up. MIDI input instead goes straight from received pitch `d1` into `input_on`; it does not automatically use the local keyboard's scale/chord mapping. [seq.c:1543–1585](https://github.com/isod89/sloop-fm1/blob/8fad1a15ff59d5c3ed0bfe2d7a9ae90962cf4d5e/firmware/src/seq.c#L1543-L1585), [seq.c:1588–1623](https://github.com/isod89/sloop-fm1/blob/8fad1a15ff59d5c3ed0bfe2d7a9ae90962cf4d5e/firmware/src/seq.c#L1588-L1623), [seq.c:2612–2627](https://github.com/isod89/sloop-fm1/blob/8fad1a15ff59d5c3ed0bfe2d7a9ae90962cf4d5e/firmware/src/seq.c#L2612-L2627).
- Pattern playback already plays the stored absolute notes directly. There is no Felucca-style `QN_SEQ` playback remapping in this Sloop snapshot. `P_QUANT` is only local input mapping (off/snap/white). [seq.c:1911–1969](https://github.com/isod89/sloop-fm1/blob/8fad1a15ff59d5c3ed0bfe2d7a9ae90962cf4d5e/firmware/src/seq.c#L1911-L1969).
- Live recording snaps to the nearest swung step with an assumed 512-sample recording-latency adjustment. `rec_note` and `rec_hit` do **not** write a captured residual into `micro`. They record tightly even though manually edited microtiming plays back. Off/Gentle is therefore real new work. [seq.c:468–557](https://github.com/isod89/sloop-fm1/blob/8fad1a15ff59d5c3ed0bfe2d7a9ae90962cf4d5e/firmware/src/seq.c#L468-L557).
- Held notes extend into TIE steps, stopping before an existing note or a full loop. A chord shares its tie duration; independent note lengths/overlapping phrases are not represented. A new note in another step ends the old recording hold. [seq.c:513–598](https://github.com/isod89/sloop-fm1/blob/8fad1a15ff59d5c3ed0bfe2d7a9ae90962cf4d5e/firmware/src/seq.c#L513-L598).
- `seq_tick` plays at most one new step per render block, retains sequence order when nudges cross, and tracks absolute steps to avoid repeats at wraps. `micro_units` uses 1/64 of the currently evaluated swung segment; negative early offsets are evaluated in the segment preceding their target step. Preserve or explicitly revise and test that normalization consistently in recording and playback. [seq.c:2026–2122](https://github.com/isod89/sloop-fm1/blob/8fad1a15ff59d5c3ed0bfe2d7a9ae90962cf4d5e/firmware/src/seq.c#L2026-L2122).
- Sloop's undo snapshot has steps and loop length only. It omits microtiming, locks and fill conditions. Once recording starts writing microtiming, an unchanged undo is incomplete. [seq.c:443–465](https://github.com/isod89/sloop-fm1/blob/8fad1a15ff59d5c3ed0bfe2d7a9ae90962cf4d5e/firmware/src/seq.c#L443-L465), [ui.c:229–252](https://github.com/isod89/sloop-fm1/blob/8fad1a15ff59d5c3ed0bfe2d7a9ae90962cf4d5e/firmware/src/ui.c#L229-L252).

## Concrete MVP

Default to two bars, 4/4, 1/16 division, 32 steps; retain supported 16/32/64-step choices (one/two/four bars). Internally preserve Sloop's synth0..2/drums3 ordering even if the UI presents Drums/Bass/Chords/Lead. Use Bass=0, Chords=1, Lead=2, Drums=3 unless the audio plan chooses another explicit map. Avoid whole-engine ID or track-order changes for branding.

The first complete phrase is a drum groove already playing, chord capture armed for the next bar, two chord gestures over two bars, capture closes automatically, then bass capture and live lead. Add a one-bar count-in when starting from stopped. Chord/bass capture should use an explicit selected-part take with a snapshot before its first write, not unexpectedly keep recording every loop forever. Drums may use deliberate overdub. Undo returns to the previous selected-part phrase. During playback, newly played notes are monitored immediately; timing correction changes the stored performance only.

Stay within the existing step model for this milestone. Do not describe it as unlimited or fully free polyphonic phrase recording: starts are represented by a common offset per step, not one offset per note/lane, and pitched note durations are shared TIE chains. At 120 BPM and zero swing one micro unit is about 1.95 ms; its duration varies with tempo/swing. Off preserves timing to this representation and event-block resolution, not analog-continuous time. Repeated hits of the same lane in one step need ratchets; distinct arbitrary hit times within that step are not represented.

## New modules and integration seams

`jb_assist.h/.c`: portable, allocation-free configuration and pure pitch/record-target helpers. APIs should return output notes; they should not mutate stored patterns, global `P_QUANT`, or a held key's already-resolved notes.

Suggested shapes (interfaces to implement, not existing APIs):

```c
typedef struct { uint8_t count, note[4]; } jb_resolved_notes_t;
jb_resolved_notes_t jb_assist_resolve(const jb_assist_t *,
    uint8_t part, uint8_t source_policy, uint8_t input_pitch);
jb_record_target_t jb_record_target(const jb_clock_snapshot_t *,
    uint8_t part, uint8_t strength, uint16_t latency_samples);
```

`jb_input.h/.c`: source ownership and one place for assist resolution. Local keys call it where `key_down` currently calls `kb_map/chord_play_notes`; MIDI calls it where `events_block` currently calls `input_on(t,d1,d2)`. Engine-specific drum/sample maps bypass harmonic assistance. A source policy distinguishes local scale-degree mapping, assisted MIDI pitch snapping, and optional raw MIDI; a MIDI note number is not a physical white-key index. Default MIDI can be raw for DAW playback, but that choice must be explicit and visible; if set assisted, local and MIDI use the same tonal rules.

Master Assist gates each part's saved preferences. Switching master off does not clear the choices. New note-on resolves the currently effective configuration once. Note-off releases the original resolved notes, original part, and original MIDI-out channel even if master Assist, root, scale, octave, chord recipe or selected part changed while held. For the first palette, chord assistance selects triads/sevenths/ninths; the scale helper uses the existing scale bitmask and predictable downward snap or white-key degree mapping. Make the selected layout clear; do not silently assume every black key is a fresh scale degree in chord mode.

Arp remapping is another seam: Sloop intentionally remaps latched physical-key notes through current scale/chord settings in `arp_next`. Keep arp out of the first MVP, or feed it immutable resolved inputs while Assist is active. Otherwise toggling Assist can change an already-held arpeggio despite the intended new-input-only rule.

`jb_record.h/.c`: arm/capture/finish state, recording strength, source filtering, and per-take undo transactions. Hook into `rec_note`, `rec_hit`, `rec_hold`, and `rec_release` without adding a second transport.

1. Snapshot the musical clock and account for known source latency in that same clock. Keep host-test latency zero; don't blindly reuse physical `REC_LAT=512` for browser controls or incoming timestamped MIDI. Derive/audition adapter offsets separately.
2. Choose the nearest actual swung grid start, including the preceding/next step across a beat or loop boundary, and calculate its signed residual. This requires a helper that can evaluate a compensated clock position; the old threshold-only `rec_target` is insufficient for recording Off.
3. Apply strength to the residual at write time: Off = 100% of residual, Gentle = 50%, Tight = zero. Quantize the result to the representable signed micro range, with symmetric rounding. Store it only when a fresh step/take owns the onset. Never overwrite an occupied step's timing merely because a new overdub note had another assistance setting.
4. Match `micro_units` exactly: an early negative residual is measured relative to the preceding swung segment; a late positive residual relative to its current segment. A golden record/playback test at swing extremes is required. Do not calculate an offset against a straight grid and then swing it again.
5. Keep swing in `grid_swing`, as a playback setting, independent of master Assist. Existing pattern bytes remain unchanged when swing changes.
6. Retain `rskip_abs` and note/lane skip handling, revised to use the newly scheduled onset: monitoring a newly recorded note must not cause a second attack when that same step fires a block later.

Existing step limits mean an overdub added to an occupied step must share its onset. Show this as step overdub behavior; independently delayed snare/hat hits at the same step require a later schema change. The current full-four-note behavior silently replaces the last note; for an assisted chord track choose an explicit one-chord-per-step replacement transaction or reject overflow visibly, rather than silently retaining three old chord tones plus one new tone.

`jb_note_router.h/.c`: note ownership across live sources and sequencer. Sloop's `seq_release` calls `trk_note_off` directly and local `input_off` does too; pitch-only release is not enough to guarantee overlapping live/playback ownership. Introduce claims/releases or equivalent checks so release of one owner cannot silence a note still held by another owner. This is a required test contract, not a claim that all upstream overlap cases have been reproduced as bugs.

Reuse local source arrays; add a fixed 24-slot resolved MIDI token pool, each roughly 12 bytes (port/channel/raw key/original part/flags/original output channel/count/resolved notes, with an explicit struct-size assertion). Add `live_ref[3][128]` at 384 bytes and pattern-owned bitsets `[3][16]` at 48 bytes, plus 27 original local MIDI-out-channel bytes. This is approximately 0.75–1 KiB additional routing state before any separate MIDI-out refcounts; actual layout and total BSS must be compiled/measured. Tokens need a source/port identity, not only channel/note, because USB and TRS may hold the same pitch. Pool-full behavior should refuse a new transformed gesture visibly and remain releasable; never lose a note-off mapping or fall back silently to an out-of-key root.

Preserve sustain ownership until the pedal releases; repeated note-on for the same source key first closes its old token. Synth all-notes-off clears tokens, reference counts, strum/pending note queues and pattern claims. Muting, stopping, undo, preset/engine changes and mode switching need explicit policies: transport stop releases pattern owners while live keys can remain held; panic releases every owner. Defer strum to the next milestone unless its delayed events are tagged with an owner generation and cancel correctly on release/mode changes. Do not turn off one shared pitch prematurely.

MIDI ingress detail: Sloop tags TRS realtime clock packets with cable 1, but normal UART channel messages at `midi_uart.c:65–69` have no port tag. Preserving USB/TRS identity requires a queue-ingress change (an internal source field/tag, with USB normalized distinctly); parsing the current packet's high cable nibble alone cannot recover information already discarded. [UART packing](https://github.com/isod89/sloop-fm1/blob/8fad1a15ff59d5c3ed0bfe2d7a9ae90962cf4d5e/firmware/src/midi_uart.c#L64-L71), [USB ingress](https://github.com/isod89/sloop-fm1/blob/8fad1a15ff59d5c3ed0bfe2d7a9ae90962cf4d5e/firmware/src/usb.c#L746-L760).

`jb_undo.h/.c`: one selected-part snapshot containing the 640-byte step union, 64-byte micro array, 16-byte fill array, 96-byte lock array, loop length/division and settings touched by the operation (roughly 832 bytes plus small metadata). This extends Sloop's existing 640-byte snapshot by around 200 bytes. A take or one knob gesture is one transaction. Undo must not rewind unrelated tracks. Commit/undo commands must cross to the audio side at a controlled block boundary; never copy a half-updated pattern under the ISR. Preserve stored pitches, microtiming and ties exactly after Assist toggles.

## Loop boundary behavior to specify and test

- Chord at step 0, ties through step 15, second chord at step 16, ties through step 31: replay twice; no duplicate live-record attack on the captured pass.
- Note beginning near step 31 and held through step 0: tie into the new cycle until release or the next NOTE, bounded to one loop. A new NOTE on step 0 retriggers by the established rule; an all-TIE pattern with no preceding NOTE must not invent a note.
- Negative micro on step 0 can be due in the previous cycle. Use absolute step IDs and `rskip_abs`; modulo loop indices alone cannot distinguish two passes.
- Swing edits while running can make the grid momentarily retreat; preserve Sloop's `seq_abs` guard. A held note should retain correct ownership throughout, even if its next step timing changes.
- Clamp/cancel impossible gates and close pending recording holds on stop, capture-end, panic and project load. No wraparound unsigned duration should become an effectively permanent note.
- Raw chromatic input with Assist off remains chromatic. A C-major recording remains exactly the same MIDI pitches and micro bytes after selecting another key and re-enabling Assist; only future playing changes.

## JAM1 storage metadata sanity check

Sloop's source explicitly says FUN6 is 3816 bytes; its `project_t` has a final 32-bit checksum, four tracks, and a packed byte per step containing six micro bits and two fill bits. [project.c:9–11](https://github.com/isod89/sloop-fm1/blob/8fad1a15ff59d5c3ed0bfe2d7a9ae90962cf4d5e/firmware/src/project.c#L9-L11), [project.c:44–80](https://github.com/isod89/sloop-fm1/blob/8fad1a15ff59d5c3ed0bfe2d7a9ae90962cf4d5e/firmware/src/project.c#L44-L80).

The proposed new JAM1 format can replace the magic and size, insert exactly 24 bytes before the checksum, and end at 3840 bytes: global eight bytes (version, master flag, palette ID, policy ID, deterministic seed `u32`) plus four bytes per track (preference flags, timing strength, chord recipe, input-source policy). Existing root/scale fields stay. Static-assert exact host/target sizes and offsets and explicitly encode endianness; no implicit ABI-dependent dump. Hash/checksum must cover the extension and new header. Reject unsupported schema versions and range-invalid values. Empty/default states must be specified, including Assist-off preserving preference flags.

This consumes the full storage-object payload. It does not leave room for free-note events or additional persistent automation. If engine-specific patch data lives in companion records upstream, preserve and validate those records too; the 24-byte extension alone is not a complete project migration. Preserve foreign saved objects/identify incompatibility; never pretend other forks' FUN6 magic means the same schema. No automatic foreign project import is implied by this plan.

## Comparisons worth borrowing carefully

Felucca: excellent explicit source-note retention and overlap tests in `chord.c`, `midi_control.c`, `tests/chord_test.c`; its most recent chord implementation is not the same as Sloop's. Felucca additionally has playback-scale quantization `QN_SEQ/SEQT` and a playback timing switch `P_SQNT` that ignores stored nudge. Those must not be advertised as an input-only Assist toggle. [Felucca chord ownership](https://github.com/hugelton/Felucca/blob/1727537624390cc3f9c7dff2456b43b3a8edb005/firmware/src/chord.c#L3-L27), [Felucca playback pitch transform](https://github.com/hugelton/Felucca/blob/1727537624390cc3f9c7dff2456b43b3a8edb005/firmware/src/seq.c#L927-L964).

Ghoulbox retains Felucca's step/input structure and adds thematic scales. Use it as evidence for a focused sound/scale identity, not a second sequencer to merge wholesale. [Ghoulbox scales](https://github.com/jasonpersinger/ghoulbox-fm1-dungeon-synth/blob/2f001b1f550eac695d8400f9f388c8eac6dbb4c6/firmware/src/seq.c#L16-L45).

ChoralRoot: portable no-allocation engine/looper, 96 PPQN clock, 512 semantic chord gestures at eight RAM bytes each, layers and undo. It records absolute root + chord quality/extensions, then reapplies voicing/performance/bass at playback. This is intentionally different from immutable resolved-note recording. Do not transplant it and claim Assist can never affect replayed harmony. Its compact duration event model is useful for a future new JamBox phrase format, after RAM/storage budgets are redesigned. [cr_loop.h:3–27](https://github.com/Quixotic7/ChoralRootFM1/blob/aa5875af20e3046ee161f988c5d91d3ae66d0c74/firmware/src/cr_loop.h#L3-L27), [event schema](https://github.com/Quixotic7/ChoralRootFM1/blob/aa5875af20e3046ee161f988c5d91d3ae66d0c74/firmware/src/cr_loop.h#L64-L82), [quantize-on-commit implementation](https://github.com/Quixotic7/ChoralRootFM1/blob/aa5875af20e3046ee161f988c5d91d3ae66d0c74/firmware/src/cr_loop.c#L491-L503).

## Validation executed

Compiled and ran ChoralRoot's existing standalone native `tests/cr_loop_test.c` with `firmware/src/cr_loop.c` and `cr_engine.c`: **471 passed, 0 failed**. Used GCC 14.2.0. The repository's strict flags initially failed at `cr_loop.c:523` on signedness in a conditional expression. Reran with only `-Wno-error=sign-compare` added; warning retained, no source edits. Build log: `deep/cr-loop-build.log`; test log: `deep/cr-loop-test.log`; binary: `deep/cr_loop_test`. This validates upstream ChoralRoot logic on the host, not proposed JamBox changes or physical FM-1 behavior.

Parallel audio research also ran Sloop's existing `seq2_test` and `scale_test` successfully using generated headers outside the checkout. Their logs were inspected here: `deep/audio-tests/sloop/seq2_test.log` ends `seq2: all checks ok`; `scale_test.log` reports all 16 scales/12 roots, octave/transpose ranges, bypass/drums/SNAP, silent keys, arp, recording/MIDI-out and held-note changes passing. These validate existing behavior, not the planned Off/Gentle write-time quantization or new ownership router.

Required future JamBox tests: two-chord capture/replay, write-time Off/Gentle/Tight, source latency at step/loop edges, swing record/playback equivalence, full step/pool behavior, chord overlap, toggle-held release, MIDI/local duplicate pitch, sustain/channel/port changes, live note vs pattern note ownership, undo restoring micro/locks/fill, round-trip JAM1/invalid metadata, and matching native/WASM scripted audio/event traces. Test actual hardware ISR headroom under all four parts before saying the voice budget is safe.
